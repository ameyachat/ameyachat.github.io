About

Hardware first, with enough software to make the hardware useful.

Background

I study Electrical and Computer Engineering at Carnegie Mellon, after a year of Computer Engineering at Purdue. My interest is hardware and embedded systems: projects where a circuit, a firmware loop, and a data pipeline all have to agree with each other.

I have built sensors for a Baja SAE race car, a noise-level board for a RoboMaster competition robot, and a wearable that shows deaf users where sounds come from. I am also part of Formula SAE at Carnegie Mellon.

This summer

Systems and Autonomy Engineering Intern at Sea Machines Robotics in Boston. I built a Python pipeline that checks radar, AIS, and camera tracks for jumps and spoofing, and I installed and tested an IMU over CAN bus on an autonomous test vessel.

On the bench

  • Molded and soldered a Hall effect wheel speed sensor, then verified voltage thresholds and pulse timing on an oscilloscope.
  • Took a noise-level sensor from breadboard to schematic, PCB layout, and soldering, read by an STM32 ADC.
  • Designed buzzer driver PCBs and tracked down a grounding fault during bring-up.

Wearable, built at StarkHacks

HearLink

A necklace for deaf and hard-of-hearing users that listens in 360 degrees. Four microphones locate and classify sounds in real time, then point to them with a display and haptic buzzers.

550 msend-to-end latency
70%accuracy over 521 classes
7 degdirection error
